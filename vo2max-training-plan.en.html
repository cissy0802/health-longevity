TRAINING PROGRAM

VO₂max Training Plan
8 Weeks · 3–4 Sessions a Week

8 weeks · 3–4 sessions weekly · running / cycling / rowing / uphill walking · includes a deload and a test week
This plan's stance: among modifiable health measures, cardiorespiratory fitness (VO₂max) is one of the strongest correlates of all-cause mortality: in large cohorts, the gap between the lowest-fitness group and a moderately fit one exceeds the gap attached to smoking, hypertension or diabetes individually. That is observational data and cannot be read straight as causation, but the direction is consistent. The good news is that it is trainable: 10–15% in eight weeks is common for someone starting sedentary. The method is no mystery either — a lot of easy aerobic work plus a little genuinely hard interval work, roughly 80 / 20.

About 80% of your total time should feel easy. The usual failure isn't training too little — it's turning every session into "moderately tired", an intensity that neither accumulates base volume nor reaches what stimulates VO₂max. It loses both ways.

A simple way to measure your real max: after 3–4 weeks of training, run a 4×4 and look at the peak heart rate in the last 30 seconds of the final interval. That number is already close to your true max, and recalculating zones from it beats any formula.

SESSION A · BASE
Zone 2 · the dullest session, and the biggest share of the plan
ItemHow
IntensityFull sentences possible, singing not. RPE 4–5, around 60–70% HRmax. Being able to hold it breathing through the nose is a good sign
Duration45–60 continuous minutes. Under 30 minutes the return drops off sharply — the point of this session is that it is long
ModalityRun, bike, row, elliptical, uphill walking. If your knees hurt or you carry more weight, favour cycling, rowing or walking
Did you get it right?You finish thinking "I could do another 20 minutes". Finishing tired means it was too hard
Most people run Zone 2 too fast. If you're worried you're going too slowly, you're probably about right. This session produces no sense of heroism; it raises mitochondrial density and fat oxidation, which are the ceiling on what your interval sessions can do.
SESSION B · KEY
4×4 intervals · the best-evidenced prescription for raising VO₂max
Session structure
SegmentDurationIntensity
Warm-up10 minEasy building to slightly breathless; finish with three 20-second pickups
Intervals × 44 min each85–95% HRmax; RPE 8–9; 2–3 words at a time is all you can manage
Recoveries × 33 min eachDon't stand still — jog or spin easily until heart rate falls to about 70%
Cool-down5 minVery easy
Choosing the pace: the first interval should go at a speed you are confident you can hold for all four. Blasting the first and falling apart on the last two is the most common and least productive way to run this session — the goal is accumulating time in the high-intensity zone, not one heroic rep.
Heart rate lags. Not being in the target zone during the first minute is normal — don't speed up because of it. Judge by breathing and RPE; use heart rate as after-the-fact confirmation.
TRY IT
Find a hill that takes four minutes to climb (or set a treadmill to 4–6%, or crank the trainer resistance). The gradient pushes intensity up for you while keeping speed and landing impact down — safer than flat-ground sprinting if your knees or ankles complain, or if you've been away from training a while.
SESSION C · OPTIONAL
30/15 short intervals · the second hard session from week 6
SegmentHow
Warm-up10 min, as for 4×4
Main set30 s fast + 15 s easy, 12–13 times per block; 2–3 blocks with 3 easy minutes between
IntensityThe 30-second efforts are faster than 4×4 but not sprints — you must hold the same speed for all 12
Cool-down5 min
Short intervals produce VO₂max gains close to 4×4, but they are psychologically easier to survive — each effort is only 30 seconds. If the thought of four-minute intervals keeps you from leaving the house, swap the weekly session for this one: adherence is worth more than the theoretically optimal protocol.
Leave at least 48 hours between hard sessions, with no heavy leg work in between. More is not better: above 2–3 hard sessions a week most amateur trainees land in the state where nothing improves and everything aches.

Don't read a watch VO₂max like a lab report. It is inferred from heart rate and pace, and individual error against the laboratory standard (graded test with gas analysis) can reach ±10–15%; strap accuracy, gradient and temperature all shift it. Read the trend, not the absolute value — same watch, same route, rising over several weeks means you genuinely improved.

⚠️ Safety & Myths

Myth 1: "HIIT is time-efficient, so do only HIIT." — People who only go hard typically improve for 3–4 weeks and then stall, with higher rates of injury and burnout. Base volume determines how much hard work you can absorb; the two aren't competitors.
Myth 2: "220 − age is my max heart rate." — Errors of ±10–12 bpm are routine. A "Zone 2" derived from it may actually be your Zone 3, which turns every session into moderate-intensity work.
Myth 3: "My watch says 42, so my VO₂max is 42." — It is an estimate with meaningful error. Comparing across brands means even less.
Myth 4: "Higher is always better, so max it out." — The return is largest at the bottom: moving from the lowest fitness quintile to the next one is associated with a far bigger risk reduction than going from good to excellent. If you are sedentary now, these first eight weeks are the best-value training you will ever do.
Get assessed first if: you are long-sedentary and over 40, you have known cardiovascular disease or several risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, early family heart disease, smoking), or you have had chest pain or fainting during exercise. Stop and seek care for chest pain or tightness, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or blacking out, or a markedly irregular heartbeat during training (see Day 63).

❓ FAQ

I can't fit a 60-minute Zone 2 session in.
Count the total instead: two 30-minute sessions and one 60-minute session are much of a muchness. Brisk commuting, stairs and dog walks all count — the Zone 2 bar is as low as "sustained brisk walking, slightly breathless". What you must protect is the one 4×4 per week.
Should I lift as well?
Yes. The adaptations don't conflict, and for longevity, strength and muscle mass are a separate pillar (see Day 12). Scheduling: keep lifting days off the hard interval days, or put lifting after intervals on the same day so the intervals happen on fresh legs.
My knees are bad. Can I still train VO₂max?
Yes. VO₂max doesn't care about modality — cycling, rowing, elliptical, swimming and uphill walking all reach the required intensity with far less impact than running. Pick whatever lets you complete four hard minutes pain-free. For the knee pain itself, see the Chronic Pain Plan.
How much can eight weeks give me?
The lower you start, the more you gain. 10–15% is common from sedentary; a trained person might see 3–5% and needs more careful periodisation. Don't measure yourself against someone else's percentage.
What happens after the eight weeks?
Maintaining is much easier than gaining: keep one hard session and two Zone 2 sessions a week and VO₂max holds. To keep climbing, run another eight-week block and add a little to base duration or interval count — rather than making every session harder.
